## The three largest stages

The Excel job is 83% of all seconds (median 17.4 s of an 18.3 s answer), and its **draw** stage is the largest
stage by far: 64% of all seconds, median 13.6 s per answer, made of the data sheet (4.7 s), the Sources sheet
(2.9 s), the answer sheet (2.7 s, of which page setup 1.6 s) and the charts (1.0 s). Second is **prepare** (17%,
median 1.3 s): the EPM fetch itself is small (prepare.fetch, median 1.1 s), and most of prepare's seconds are b16's
ledger drill (prepare.drill, median 7.3 s on b16). Third is **save** of the workbook (11%, median 2.4 s). Opening
the target is 0.2 s. An answer makes 259 to 377 COM calls.

Two effects to keep in mind when comparing later phases:
- The workbook grows during the bench (1 to 19 answers), and the answers slow down round by round: the median wall
  time of the five ok cases without a drill (b16 left out) was 13.6 s in round 1, 17.7 s in round 2 and 29.3 s in
  round 3 (from the script's progress lines). The slowdown is all in the Excel job, with the same 261 COM calls
  (median) in each round, so each call gets slower as the workbook grows. Same five cases, medians from their
  run-log lines:

  | round | excel job s | draw s | draw.data sheet s | draw.sources s | draw.answer s | save s | prepare s |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 1 | 12.7 | 9.9 | 3.6 | 2.4 | 2.3 | 1.3 | 1.3 |
  | 2 | 16.2 | 12.5 | 4.3 | 2.8 | 2.7 | 2.4 | 1.0 |
  | 3 | 27.8 | 18.9 | 5.9 | 4.4 | 3.3 | 4.6 | 1.4 |

- The warm-up (67.0 s, of which the Excel job 61.7 s) includes starting Excel (none was running) and creating the
  workbook; b16's first drill included the drill browser's start (3.9 s) and sign-in (6.2 s), which a running
  server has warm.
